Two people give the same advice in the same meeting. "We should delay the launch by a week."

When the first one says it, the room nods and someone starts rearranging the calendar. When the second says it, there's a polite pause, and the conversation moves on as if nobody spoke.

Same words, same logic, same room. What was different?

Everything that arrived *before* the words did.

## The side we've been skipping

Back in Week 1 we met the Success Triangle: self-mastery, communication, observation.

For seven weeks we've trained observation and communication. We can hear what people need and say what lands. But the triangle has a third side, and it decides whether anyone trusts what we say in the first place.

That side is self-mastery, and the form it takes in other people's eyes is **authority**.

So this week we stop reading other people and read ourselves.

## Five traits

The model we'll use rates five traits. Each runs on five levels, from struggling at level 1 to *contagious* at level 5, where the trait is so settled in us that it rubs off on the people around us.

| Trait | The question it asks |
| --- | --- |
| **Confidence** | How settled are we in ourselves when we speak up, get praised, or get challenged? |
| **Discipline** | Do the things we intend and the things we do match? |
| **Leadership** | What happens to the people around us: do they shrink, or do they grow? |
| **Gratitude** | Where does our attention go first: to what's wrong, or to what's good? |
| **Enjoyment** | How at ease are we on an ordinary day, and what does that do to a room? |

That last trait surprises people. Why would enjoyment be part of authority?

Think about who we instinctively trust. It's rarely the tense, guarded person. It's the one who seems settled, whose mood doesn't swing with the meeting. Ease reads as strength.

## Five habit areas

Traits don't float in mid-air. They rest on habits, and the model names five areas, each rated 1 to 5:

- **Environment:** the state of the spaces we live and work in
- **Time:** how we handle our hours and our promises
- **Appearance:** how we look after and present ourselves
- **Social:** the company we keep and how we show up in it
- **Financial:** how we manage money

It's a bit like a house. The traits are the rooms people see. The habits are the wiring and plumbing. Nobody compliments the plumbing, but everyone notices when it fails.

## Three rules for using the scores

**1. The goal sits one level up.** Wherever we rate ourselves, the target is the next level, not level 5. A level 2 doesn't try to act like a level 5. A level 2 works on becoming a 3.

**2. Lowest first.** We work on our lowest trait and our lowest habit area before anything else.

**3. Fixing a weakness beats building a strength.** This is the key principle, and it runs against instinct. We like polishing what we're already good at. But our lowest trait has the biggest effect on how much influence we have.

Picture a bucket with five staves of different heights. It only holds water up to the shortest one. Making the tall staves taller does nothing.

## How to be honest

A self-rating is only as good as its honesty, and honesty about ourselves is hard. Three things help.

Rate the **last month**, not our best day. Rate what we **do**, not what we intend. And where we hesitate between two levels, take the lower one. If we truly belonged at the higher level, we wouldn't be hesitating.

## One more look at that meeting

Go back to the two people and the delayed launch.

The first had shown up on time for a year, kept her promises, given credit away, and stayed calm when things went wrong. The room had been rating her authority all along, without a form.

People are doing the same with us. This week we find out what they're seeing.
