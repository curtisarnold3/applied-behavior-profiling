Most of us ask "how are you?" the way we say "excuse me." It's a noise we make on the way past.

Nora doesn't. She asks, we say "fine," and she tilts her head.

"No. How are you *really* doing?"

And then she waits. She actually wants to know, and she'll stand there in the hallway until we tell her.

## The end goal

Nora's value is **connection**: emotional closeness, belonging, and being truly seen.

It isn't about the spotlight. People with this value don't need to be the center of the room. They need the room to be close. If the choice is between being admired from a distance and being known up close, it isn't a choice.

## Where it often comes from

Values tend to grow from what was missing, and connection often grows from **emotional distance** early on.

A home where everyone was polite and nobody talked. Parents who provided everything except closeness. The child from that house frequently becomes the adult who makes sure nobody around them ever feels that far away.

## How it shows up

- They check in often, and not only when something's wrong.
- They send long, caring messages.
- They want everyone to belong, and they notice the person standing at the edge of the group.
- They ask real questions and remember the answers.

## What to listen for

<Phrases>
- "How are you *really* doing?"
- "I just want people to feel cared for."
- "Ultimately, I just want us to be close."
</Phrases>

Listen for the adjectives, too. People who value connection admire those who are *warm*, *present*, *genuine*.

## How to move them

With a connection person, the way forward is to **close the emotional distance**.

That means real presence. Put the phone down. Answer the actual question. Share something true.

What doesn't work is pressure, and neither does surface talk. A slick pitch delivered at arm's length will fail with Nora even if every word is right, because the distance itself is the problem.

If we need to ask something of her, we connect first and ask second. And we frame the request around closeness: "I'd love to do this *with* you."

## Connection or acceptance?

They sound alike, so here's the difference.

**Acceptance** is a need. It's about being let into *this group*, now. It can be met by a seat at the table.

**Connection** is a value. It's the long-run goal of real closeness, and a seat at the table doesn't satisfy it. Nora could belong to a dozen groups and still feel she's missing the thing she wants, if nobody in them really knows her.

One is about being included. The other is about being known.
