We've been on hold for eleven minutes when a human finally picks up.

"I do apologize for the wait. I'm afraid our policy is that refunds have to be requested within thirty days, and you're at thirty-four. That's just how the system's set up."

A month ago we'd have heard an obstacle. Now, do we hear something else?

*Policy. How the system's set up.* That's a justification, and it's the conformity pillar, plain as day.

## Pillars are everywhere

That call is the point of this lesson. We don't need a boardroom or a big negotiation to practice. Decision pillars show up anywhere a person explains a choice, and people explain choices all day long.

So what do we do with the agent on the phone?

Arguing against the policy is a deviance move, and it's the one most likely to fail with a conformity decider. Instead we stay inside the frame: "I understand, and I'd like to do this the right way. Is there a standard exception process for cases just outside the window?"

We've asked for the proper procedure. For someone who decides by rules, that's a request they can say yes to.

## All six on one page

| Pillar | They decide by | We hear | Frame it as | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Deviance | Doing the opposite of what's expected | "Why do we even do it this way?" | Breaking the pattern | "Everyone's doing it" |
| Novelty | What's new and different | "I've never tried that" | Fresh, first, an experiment | "Tried and true" |
| Social | The effect on people close to them | "Is everyone okay with this?" | Good for the group | Cold data, pushing |
| Conformity | Rules, structure, what others do | "Technically we're supposed to..." | Standard, proven | Disruption |
| Investment | What they've already put in | "I've put so much into this" | Paying off the effort | "Starting over" |
| Necessity | What has to happen now | "What's the bottom line?" | Needed now; the cost of waiting | Too many options |

## The two pairs to keep straight

**Deviance and novelty.** Deviance wants different *from the crowd*. Novelty wants *new*. Mention that something's trending: novelty leans in, deviance leans out.

**Social and conformity.** Social protects a *small circle* of named people. Conformity follows the *larger culture* and its rules. Listen for names versus norms.

## One request, three ways

The deliverable this week is the best practice we know for pillars: take one real request and write it three different ways.

Let's try it. The request: we'd like a colleague to review our report by Thursday.

**For an investment decider:** "You helped shape the first draft, so your fingerprints are already on this. A quick look by Thursday means that early work pays off."

**For a necessity decider:** "This goes to the client Friday morning. I need your eyes on section two by Thursday, or it goes out unchecked."

**For a social decider:** "The whole team's name is on this one. Could you look it over by Thursday so nobody gets caught out in the meeting?"

Three true sentences about the same favor. Which one we send depends entirely on who's reading it.

Notice, too, what we didn't do. We didn't make anything up. Each version picks a real feature of the situation and puts it first.

## The formula disappears

Right now this feels like a formula. Listen for the justification, name the pillar, check the table, build the sentence.

That's how it's supposed to feel in week five. It was the same with needs, and with driving a car.

With practice, the steps fade. We stop thinking "that's conformity" and simply find ourselves saying, "what's the proper way to handle this?" The adjustment becomes automatic, and what's left is just a conversation that goes well.

## One layer left

We now have two of the three layers.

**Needs** tell us what someone is chasing from other people. **Pillars** tell us how they decide.

What's missing is the destination. Underneath the chasing and the deciding, what is this person ultimately trying to reach? What would they give other things up for?

That's the third layer, **values**, and it's where we go next.
