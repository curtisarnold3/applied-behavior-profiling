It's trivia night, and our team has just written down "Sydney" for the capital of Australia.

Dev leans in. "Actually, it's Canberra. Common mistake. Sydney and Melbourne both wanted it, so they built a new city between them. I listened to a whole podcast on this."

He's right, and we get the point. But we also got a history lecture nobody ordered. What was that for?

## What intelligence wants

Dev is showing the **intelligence** need: to be seen as smart, competent, and insightful.

There's a small phrase in that sentence doing a lot of work: *seen as*. A need is something we want from other people. It's not a measure of what we are. Someone with this need may or may not be the brightest person in the room. What matters is that they need us to think so.

The hidden fear follows from that. It's being seen as stupid, or uninformed, or caught not knowing.

## What it sounds like

The tone is precise, corrective, and anchored in data.

<Phrases>
- "Most people don't understand this."
- "I don't want to sound arrogant, but I've done a lot of research."
- "Logically, it doesn't follow."
- "I read a study on this."
- "Well, technically..."
</Phrases>

The single best tell is one word at the start of a sentence: **"Actually."** It's a tiny flag that says a correction is coming, and a correction is a chance to be the one who knows.

## What it can look like

A few visual hints, held loosely as always. Clothing tends toward minimalist, neutral, and carefully chosen. Sometimes there's intellectual signaling: the clever T-shirt, the book carried cover-out, the specialized gear.

In conversation, we might see a classic thinking pose, very exact word choices, and self-correction mid-sentence to get a detail right. They ask detailed questions. There can be a bit of emotional distance, and when their thinking is challenged, they may wave the challenger off.

Clothing in particular tells us little here. Lots of people simply like gray sweaters. The speech pattern is far more reliable.

## Language that lands

Our instinct with Dev is to argue, or to show him we're clever too. Both turn the conversation into a contest, and a person with this need can't afford to lose it.

What works is anchoring in **logic, strategy, and insight**, and making sure the insight is *theirs*.

- "You were the first person I thought of, because this one's complicated."
- "Walk me through how you'd think about it."
- "That's a distinction I hadn't considered."

Then watch. When we praise their insight, they relax. The corrections slow down, because the thing they were working for has been handed over.

<Callout label="Whose insight?">
Notice the difference between "that's smart" and "here's something smart I know." The first feeds the need. The second competes with it.
</Callout>

## Intelligence or significance?

These two get mixed up, because both can sound like showing off.

Significance is about **rank**: I'm above average. "I can't stand mediocrity."

Intelligence is about **knowing**: I understand what others don't. "Most people don't get how this works."

If the brag is about being better, think significance. If it's about being correct, think intelligence. And if we hear both, we speak to both.

## The superpower

People with the intelligence need have spent years collecting knowledge and getting it exactly right. That makes them **outstanding teachers**.

Ask one of them to explain something and we'll often get the clearest explanation we've ever heard, delivered with real pleasure. Dev's Canberra lecture was unrequested. It was also pretty good.
