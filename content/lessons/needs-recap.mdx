Picture a campfire some twenty thousand years ago. Thirty people, give or take, and beyond the firelight a great deal of dark.

In that circle, what the others thought of us wasn't a matter of feelings. It decided whether we ate, whether we were protected, whether anyone carried us when we broke an ankle. Being thrown out of the group was, more or less, a death sentence.

That's the short answer to a fair question: why these six?

## Why these six

Each of the six needs maps to something that, in a small ancestral tribe, affected whether a person survived or had children.

Take pity. A sick or injured person whom nobody cared for would die. Being able to draw care from the group was a survival skill.

The others follow the same logic. Someone the tribe considered important was worth protecting. Someone who belonged got a share of the food. Someone judged to be good was kept around. Someone known to be clever was listened to. Someone respected as strong wasn't challenged.

We don't live around that fire anymore. Our nervous systems haven't entirely got the news.

## An honest caveat

These six aren't a complete list of everything a human being needs. Nobody's claiming that.

They were chosen for a practical reason: they can be **profiled**, and they can be **used**. We can hear them in ordinary conversation, and once we've heard them, we know what to say next.

It's a field tool, not a theory of everything.

## All six on one page

| Need | They crave | They fear | Language that lands |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Significance | To be seen as exceptional | Being invisible or average | Impact, achievement, exceptionality |
| Acceptance | To belong | Being left out | Inclusive, tribal, "we" language |
| Approval | To be judged good and kind | Disapproval | Specific praise for specific behaviors |
| Intelligence | To be seen as smart | Looking uninformed | Logic, strategy, their insight |
| Pity | To have hardship believed | Being alone or disbelieved | Empathy, safety, softness |
| Strength | Respect | Looking weak or dependent | Respect, resilience, independence |

The first three are the primary needs. The last three are secondary.

## The gift inside each need

It would be easy to walk away from this unit thinking of needs as flaws. They aren't. Each one also builds a strength.

- **Approval** grows empathy. Years of watching how others feel makes a person very good at it.
- **Acceptance** grows a chameleon's fit, and fierce loyalty to the tribe.
- **Intelligence** grows teachers. Nobody explains like someone who needs to get it right.
- **Strength** grows fearlessness.
- **Significance** grows an immunity to other people's judgment, and the focus that comes with it.
- **Pity** has no clearly named gift. That one stays an open question.

Keeping the gift in mind does something for us as readers of people. It's hard to look down on someone while we're appreciating what they're good at. And the moment we look down on someone, we stop being able to read them.

## Three reminders before we go on

**Read the moment.** A need is what's showing here and now, not a permanent label.

**Two is fine.** When we hear two needs, we speak to both.

**Words first.** What we see is a hint. What we hear is the evidence.

## Where we are

We can now hear six different things people reach for. There's one puzzle left in this unit.

Why does the same person seem to want significance on Tuesday at the office and acceptance on Saturday with old friends? Are we misreading them?

We're not. That's the next lesson.
