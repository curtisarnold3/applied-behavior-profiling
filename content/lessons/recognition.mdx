The project finished on time, and the director sent a note to the whole department.

It thanked the team. It thanked the client. It mentioned the launch, the numbers, and the weather on the day.

It didn't mention that Imani had stayed until midnight three nights running to fix the thing that would have sunk it.

Imani read the note, closed it, and said nothing. Two weeks later she's still thinking about it.

## The end goal

Imani's value is **recognition**: to be seen, valued, and remembered. Visibility and worth.

Notice what she didn't want. She didn't want a parade. People with this value will often say so directly: "I don't need praise." What they need is to know that the effort **registered**, that it was seen and that it counted.

## Where it often comes from

Recognition often grows from effort and presence that **went unnoticed**.

The child who did everything right and got a nod. The one who was reliably there and was treated as part of the furniture. They learned that work can vanish without a trace, and they've been trying to make it leave a mark ever since.

## How it shows up

- They notice, quickly, when effort isn't acknowledged, whether it's theirs or someone else's.
- They mention how hard they've worked, sometimes more than once.
- They remember who said thank you, and who didn't.

## What to listen for

<Phrases>
- "I don't need praise. I just want to know it mattered."
- "I just want someone to see the effort."
- "Ultimately, I just want it to have counted for something."
</Phrases>

## How to move them

Here's the key instruction: speak to their **impact**, not their intention.

"I know you meant well" is about intention. It does nothing. "Thanks for trying" is worse.

Impact sounds like this:

- "Because you caught that, the launch held."
- "People will remember what you did here."
- "That midnight fix is the reason we still have the client."

We name the specific thing and we name what it changed. The message is: *it landed, and it left a mark.*

## Recognition or significance?

This is one of the most commonly confused pairs in the whole course, so let's take it slowly.

**Significance** is a **need**. It's wanting to be seen as exceptional by other people, *now*. It's about rank, and it shows up as standing out.

**Recognition** is a **value**. It's the end goal of being seen and having it matter. It's about worth, and it shows up as wanting effort acknowledged.

Marcus, from the barbecue in Unit 2, wanted us to know he was above average. Imani doesn't care about being above anyone. She wants to know the three nights weren't invisible.

A question that separates them: *would being called "the best" satisfy this person?* For significance, yes. For recognition, not really. "The best" is a ranking. Imani wants a receipt.

## Recognition or growth?

There's a second pair worth a sentence, and we'll see it again in the growth lesson.

With recognition, we speak to **what they've done**: the impact already made. With growth, we speak to **where they're going**: the trajectory. Praise a growth person for a finished achievement and they shrug. Tell a recognition person only about their potential and they'll wonder whether we noticed what they've already given.
