At a neighborhood barbecue, someone asks Marcus what he does. Ninety seconds later we know that he runs the largest dealership in the county, that he was the first in his family to own a business, and that the mayor texts him directly.

Nobody asked about the mayor.

It's easy to roll our eyes. But let's do something more useful, and ask why he needed to say it.

## What significance wants

Marcus is showing the **significance** need. He wants to be seen as important, unique, impactful. If we're honest, a little better than the people around him.

Underneath that sits a quiet fear: being invisible. Being average. Being just another guy at a barbecue.

Once we see the fear, the bragging stops being annoying and starts being information. He's not telling us he's great. He's asking us whether we noticed.

## What it sounds like

Significance has a recognizable sound. The tone runs competitive and proud, sometimes with a slight wave of the hand at everyone else.

<Phrases>
- "I just don't want to feel average."
- "I have really high standards."
- "They didn't even recognize what I did."
- "I can't stand mediocrity."
- "People always come to me when..."
- "I was the first to..."
</Phrases>

Notice the first three. Significance doesn't always arrive as a boast. Sometimes it shows up as a complaint that nobody noticed, or as "perfectionism." The thread tying them together is rank: I'm above the ordinary, or I should be.

## What it can look like

There are things we might see, too. Visible logos and luxury pieces. An outfit that was clearly assembled to be looked at. Grooming that's immaculate in a way that asks for attention. A lifted chin, a voice a notch louder than the group, the seat at the head of the table.

Name-dropping and the humble brag belong here as well.

Hold all of that loosely. Appearance is a general signal and a weaker one than speech. Plenty of people wear a nice watch because their grandfather gave it to them. Let the eyes raise the question and let the ears answer it.

## Language that lands

So what do we say to Marcus?

The mistake is to match him, or to deflate him. Both leave the need unfed, and he'll simply turn up the volume.

What works is speaking to **impact, achievement, and exceptionality**. We let him know we see the thing he's been waving at us.

- "At your level, I'd guess most advice doesn't even apply."
- "Most people wouldn't get what you're building."
- "That's not a normal result. How'd you pull it off?"

Watch what happens next. Often the shoulders drop and the volume comes down. He doesn't have to keep proving it, because somebody finally said it.

<Callout label="Why this isn't flattery">
We're not inventing praise. We're acknowledging something he already told us matters to him. If we can't say it sincerely, we don't say it. We find the true version instead.
</Callout>

## The superpower

Every need comes with a gift, and it's worth remembering when a significance person is wearing us out.

People with this need can tune out other people's judgment and stay on mission. While the rest of us are wondering what the neighbors think, they're still building. A lot of hard things in the world got finished by someone who refused to be ordinary.
