The job offer is everything Maria wanted. More money, a bigger title, work she's been hoping to do for years.

She reads it twice, and her first words aren't about any of that.

"What would this do to my team? And the hours. I'd have to talk to Daniel about the school run."

She'll take a week to decide, and almost none of that week will be spent thinking about herself.

## How the social pillar decides

Maria uses the **social** pillar. She makes choices according to how they'll affect the people close to her: friends, family, the team.

What she's protecting is harmony and relationships. A decision that's good for her but hard on them isn't, in her accounting, a good decision.

## What it sounds like

<Phrases>
- "How will they feel about this?"
- "I don't want to hurt anyone's feelings."
- "I want everyone comfortable with this."
- "Let's make sure everyone's okay with it."
</Phrases>

The tone is gentle. The pronouns are *we*, *us*, *our team*. And notice how social deciders handle a strong opinion: they soften it. "I might be wrong, but I wonder if..." is a bold view wearing a cardigan.

## What it can look like

We might notice warm eye contact, soft open gestures, nodding, leaning in. Often they'll watch how others react before giving their own response.

These are general signals and less reliable than speech. Lots of people nod. What we're really waiting for is the justification that puts other people first.

## How to frame it

We frame the idea around **connection, harmony, and the good it does for others**.

- "This will make your team feel more supported."
- "It takes pressure off the people around you."
- "Everyone I've talked to is comfortable with it."

Tone matters as much as content here. Calm and collaborative. We're working it out together.

**What to avoid:** cold data dumps, anything that sounds like every-man-for-himself, and pushing. Too much excitement can backfire too. To a social decider, urgency sounds like someone's about to get trampled.

## An example

Suppose we're the one offering Maria that job.

The significance pitch, "you'd be the youngest director we've had," might not move her at all. The necessity pitch, "we need an answer by Friday," could actively push her away.

The social pitch: "Your team would still report to you, and I'd want your help choosing who steps into your current role, so nobody's left stranded. Take the time you need to talk to Daniel."

We've answered the question she was actually asking.

## Social or conformity?

Here's the second commonly confused pair in this unit. Both care about other people, so what's the difference?

It's the size of the circle.

**Social** looks at a **small circle**: specific, named people. My team. My family. Daniel.

**Conformity** looks at the **larger culture**: what people in general do, what's proper, what the rules say.

Listen for names versus norms. "I don't want to upset Karen and Luis" is social. "That's just not how it's done" is conformity. Maria would cheerfully break a company norm if it protected her people.

## Don't confuse it with a need

One more distinction, since this pillar sounds a lot like the acceptance and approval needs from Unit 2.

A need is what someone wants *from* people. A pillar is how they *decide*. Maria may or may not have an acceptance need. What we know for sure is that when a choice lands on her desk, she weighs it by its effect on others.

If we hear both a need and a pillar, good. That's two layers of the profile, and we speak to both.
