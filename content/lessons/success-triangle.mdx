Elena had the better product, the better price, and a slide deck she'd rehearsed four times. Forty minutes into the meeting, the client thanked her warmly and never called back.

On the drive home she ran the usual explanations. Bad timing. Tight budgets. They were never serious.

Maybe. But there's a more useful question than "what went wrong?" It's "which of three things went wrong?"

## Three sides

Any success or failure, whether it's a sale, a relationship, or a moment of leadership, comes down to three skills. All three can be trained.

<SuccessTriangle />

**Self-mastery** is how we show up. Confidence, discipline, focus, emotional control. It also covers posture, tone, timing, and presence.

**Communication** is what we say and how we say it, and just as much, how the other person feels and decides in response.

**Observation** is decoding what's actually happening in front of us: the cues, the tension, the patterns the other person doesn't know they're showing.

## What a leak looks like

When results are off, one side is leaking. Each leak has its own signature.

| Side | What failure looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Self-mastery | Overreacting. Fear-based responses. Weak boundaries. Insecurity showing through. The right words, delivered the wrong way |
| Communication | Good intentions landing as pressure or confusion. Data given to someone who needed feeling. Right thing, wrong person, wrong time |
| Observation | Missing discomfort or disengagement. Missing that they were ready to decide. Misreading needs or values. Trusting the wrong person |

Read that table again with Elena in mind.

Did she talk faster and faster as the room went quiet? That's self-mastery. Did she give forty minutes of data to someone who needed to feel understood? Communication. Did she miss the moment, twelve minutes in, when the client leaned back and stopped taking notes? Observation.

Any one of those could have sunk the meeting. And notice that none of them is "bad luck."

## A habit, not a theory

The triangle earns its keep as a diagnostic habit. When something goes wrong, we check the three sides before we reach for any other explanation.

It's a bit like a plumber with a pressure gauge. A plumber doesn't stand in a flooded kitchen and wonder about fate. There are a limited number of places a pipe can fail, and we check them in order.

The habit does something else for us, too. "They were never serious" is a story about them, and we can't train them. "I missed the moment they checked out" is a story about a skill, and skills improve.

## Where the course fits

Every skill we'll build over the next eight weeks strengthens one of these three sides.

Most of the course lives on the **observation** side: hearing needs, decision pillars, and values in everyday speech. Each of those units also teaches the matching **communication**: once we know what someone is reaching for, what do we say?

The last unit, in Week 8, goes to **self-mastery**. That's the side that decides whether anyone trusts what we say in the first place.

When an interaction goes badly, check the three sides first. Which one leaked?

## Try it on a small one

We don't need a lost sale to practice this. A text that landed wrong will do. So will a chat with a neighbor that got strangely tense.

Pick one from this week and ask the three questions. Was it how we showed up? What we said, or how it landed? Or something we didn't notice?

Usually the answer arrives quickly, and it's a little uncomfortable. That's a good sign. It means we've found something we can fix.
