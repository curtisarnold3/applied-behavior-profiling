Six friends again, the same six from the restaurant. This time it's late, the plates are cleared, and someone asks a bigger question: "If you could have one thing sorted for the rest of your life, what would it be?"

One says, "To be properly close to the people I love." One says, "To never be blindsided again." One says, after a pause, "For it to have mattered that I was here." One says, "To have really lived, not just got through." One says, "For nobody to tell me what to do." And the last says, "To keep getting better."

Nobody at the table is talking about dinner now. They're talking about where their whole lives are pointed.

## The third layer

We've built two layers so far.

**Needs** are what people chase from others. They drive behavior. **Decision pillars** are how people choose. They guide behavior.

**Values** are the end game: what a person is trying, often without knowing it, to earn, gain, or restore. They're the thing someone will give other things up for.

If the need is the fuel and the pillar is the GPS, the value is the destination that's been typed in.

## Built from what was missing

Here's the idea that makes values make sense.

Values are usually built from **deficit**, not abundance. They grow out of whatever felt missing in the years when a person was being formed.

- A kid who never felt important becomes an adult chasing **recognition**.
- A kid who never got straight answers lives for **information**.
- A kid who felt trapped fights, for the rest of their life, for **freedom**.

It's a bit like hunger. We don't think much about food after a good meal. The person who thinks about it all day is the one who went without.

That changes how we hear people. When someone talks with real longing about something, they're often telling us what they didn't get.

## Why speaking to a value is so powerful

Speaking to someone's value means saying something they've been trying to fix for years.

That's why it lands differently from an ordinary compliment. A need is met in the moment. A value, when someone touches it, reaches further down.

We should treat that with some care. We're not looking for a lever. We're looking for the thing that matters most to this person, so we can stop accidentally stepping on it and start connecting to it.

## Values that look like needs

A few values resemble needs we already know. Connection sounds like acceptance. Recognition sounds like significance.

They're different drivers. Here's the cleanest way to hold it:

> A need is a means. A value is the end goal.

Someone might chase acceptance (a need) from this group, today, as one of many ways of getting toward connection (a value) over a lifetime. The need is the step. The value is where the staircase goes.

We'll sharpen the trickiest pair, recognition and significance, in the recognition lesson.

## The listening cue for values

For needs we listened to repetitions. For pillars, justifications. For values, we listen to **adjectives**: specifically, the words people use for those they admire.

"She's so *grounded*." "He's so *free*." "They're just so *real*."

Whatever a person romanticizes in others is usually what they were deprived of. The admiration is pointing at the gap.

<Callout label="A phrase to listen for">
Values often arrive after the words "ultimately, I just want..." When we hear that opening, we lean in. What follows is usually the destination.
</Callout>
