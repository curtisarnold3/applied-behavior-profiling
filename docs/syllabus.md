# Applied Behavior Profiling: Course Syllabus

As of 2026-11-23 · Curtis A. Arnold

> Source of truth for the website's structure. Exported from the "Applied Behavior Profiling: Course Syllabus" doc.

## Course overview

This 8-week course teaches you to work out *why* people act the way they do from what they say: which social need they are chasing, how they justify decisions, and what they value most. The last unit turns the lens inward, onto your own authority, habits, and limiting beliefs.

The course treats behavior as patterned, not personal. Most adult behavior traces back to childhood strategies for earning friends, safety, and rewards. Once you can spot the pattern, you can stop reacting to the surface and respond to the person underneath it.

This course covers profiling from language and conversation. Physical behavior monitoring and deception scoring are a separate course.

**By the end of the course, you will be able to:**

1. Explain the five stages of perception (broken, different, facts, reasons, reflections) and place your own default stage.
2. Identify the six childhood wound types and the adult behaviors each one produces.
3. Diagnose a failed interaction with the Success Triangle: self-mastery, communication, or observation.
4. Identify a person's primary social need from their everyday language, test it, and respond in matching language.
5. Name the decision pillar someone uses to justify a choice and frame a proposal to fit it.
6. Recognize a person's core value and connect a request to it.
7. Combine needs, decisions, and values into a single working profile, and correct common profiling errors.
8. Score your own authority traits and habits, and build a dated plan to raise your lowest score.

## Course structure and format

The course runs 8 weeks in five units, about 3–4 hours a week: roughly 1 hour of lessons and 2–3 hours of practice. Practice matters more than lessons. The goal is pattern recognition that becomes automatic, not memorized definitions.

| Unit | Weeks | Focus | Source lessons |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Foundations | 1–2 | How people see the world, where behavior comes from | Foundations 01–06 |
| 2. Human Needs | 3–4 | The six social needs and how they shift by context | Core 01–10 |
| 3. Decision Pillars | 5 | The six filters people use to justify choices | Core 11–21 |
| 4. Values and Integration | 6–7 | The six end-goal values; building one combined profile | Core 22–32, Foundations 07–08 |
| 5. Authority and Self-Development | 8 | Your own presence, habits, and limiting beliefs | Inventory, Authority Triangle, Milgram, Limiting Beliefs |

**Three ground rules carry through every unit:**

- **Read the moment, not the life story.** Profile what a person is showing in this context. The same person can show different needs at work, with friends, and at home.
- **Two is fine.** If you see two needs, pillars, or values, speak to both. People don't fit neatly in a spreadsheet.
- **Listen for why, not what.** Ask why the person felt the need to say something, not just what they said.

**Practice runs on two tracks each week:**

1. **Controlled practice:** watch interviews, podcasts, or TV and log what you hear (need, pillar, value, the phrase that tipped you off).
2. **Live practice:** short everyday conversations where you identify, test, and respond, then write a two-line debrief afterward.

## Unit 1: Foundations (Weeks 1–2)

Unit 1 builds the lens everything else sits on: people's behavior is a strategy learned in childhood, and you can only read it well once you stop judging it.

| # | Lesson | Key ideas | Practice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.1 | The Five Lenses (intro + extended lecture) | Five stages of perception: seeing others as broken, different, facts, reasons, then reflections. The Six Wound Types: social, authority, emotional, security, validation, trust | Note which stage you defaulted to in three moments of irritation this week |
| 1.2 | The Success Triangle | Every outcome rests on three trainable skills: self-mastery, communication, observation. A failure means one side has a leak | Take one interaction that went badly and name the leaking side |
| 1.3 | The Childhood Development Triangle | Early behavior forms around friends, safety, and rewards. A shortfall in one creates a lasting adult strategy (people pleaser, controller, status seeker) | Match three people you know to the triangle side driving them |
| 1.4 | The Four Laws of Human Behavior | Everyone is suffering and insecure; everyone wears a mask; everyone pretends not to; everyone is a product of childhood suffering and reward. Profiling is mostly spotting how someone hides shame | Write down one judgment you made and restate it as a reason |
| 1.5 | How Language Matters More Than Ever | Words deliver social reward. Most persuasion fails by giving the right message to the wrong kind of person | Preview: listen for one phrase that sounds like a need |

**Key takeaway:** behavior is patterned, not personal. Judgment ends your ability to read and influence someone.

**Check for understanding:** given a short description of an adult behavior, name the likely wound and the triangle side behind it.

## Unit 2: Human Needs (Weeks 3–4)

Unit 2 teaches the six social needs: the emotional fuel a person is chasing from other people right now. You learn to hear each one, test your read, and answer it in matching language.

| Need | What you hear | What they crave | Language shift |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Significance | "I was the youngest to ever..." | To be seen as exceptional | Impact, achievement, exceptionality |
| Acceptance | "I just didn't feel part of the group" | To belong | Inclusive, tribal, "we" language |
| Approval | "I hope that's okay" / "I'm just trying to help" | To be judged good and kind | Specific praise for specific behaviors |
| Intelligence | "Actually, the way it works is..." | To be seen as smart | Logic, strategy, insight (theirs) |
| Pity | "It's just been a lot lately" | To have their hardship believed | Empathy, safety, softness |
| Strength | "I don't need anybody's help" | Respect | Respect, resilience, independence; ask permission before complimenting |

**Lessons**

- **Week 3:** Core 01 Introduction · 02 The Human Needs Map · 03 Significance · 04 Acceptance · 05 Approval
- **Week 4:** Core 06 Intelligence · 07 Pity · 08 Strength · 09 Needs Recap · 10 Four Needs Circles

The Four Needs Circles explain why one person shows different needs in different settings: inner circle (partners, mentors), legacy circle (long-time friends), proximity circle (coworkers, acquaintances), and outer circle (strangers). In the field you don't need to know the circle. Identify the need, test it, use it, move on.

**Practice:** log 10 need identifications a week, each with the exact phrase that tipped you off. In live practice, try one matching compliment and note the reaction.

**Check for understanding:** for six recorded statements, name the need and write one sentence that speaks to it.

## Unit 3: Decision Pillars (Week 5)

Unit 3 covers the six filters people use to justify a decision. Needs are what someone wants from other people; pillars are how they decide. Frame a proposal through the wrong pillar and a good idea gets rejected.

| Pillar | How they decide | What you hear | Frame your proposal as... |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Deviance | Doing the opposite of what's expected | "Why do we even do it this way?" | Breaking the rules, going against the grain |
| Novelty | What's new, exciting, different | "I've never tried that" | Fresh, first, unlike anything before |
| Social | How it affects people | "Let's make sure everyone's okay with this" | Good for the group, relationships protected |
| Conformity | Structure, rules, what others do | "Technically we're supposed to..." | Standard, proven, what others like them do |
| Investment | What they've already put in; the return | "I've already put so much into this" | Protecting and building on what they've invested |
| Necessity | Urgency, what has to happen now | "Okay, but what are we doing?" | Needed now, the cost of waiting |

**Lessons:** Core 11 Needs Overview (bridge) · 12 Decision Pillars Introduction · 13 All Decisions Walkthrough · 14 Decisions Introduction · 15 Deviance · 16 Novelty · 17 Social · 18 Conformity · 19 Investment · 20 Necessity · 21 Decisions Wrap Up

**Listening cue:** pillars show up most in justifications. When someone explains or defends a recurring behavior, you're hearing their decision filter, and often what used to feel dangerous to them.

**Practice:** take one real request you need to make this week. Write it three ways for three different pillars, then use the one that fits the person.

**Check for understanding:** match six justification statements to their pillars and rewrite one pitch for a Necessity listener.

## Unit 4: Values and Integration (Weeks 6–7)

Unit 4 adds the third layer, values: the deeper end goal a person keeps pursuing, often because it went unmet. Week 7 then combines needs, pillars, and values into one working profile.

### Week 6: Values

| Value | End goal | What you notice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Connection | Closeness, belonging | Asks "how are you really doing?" and means it |
| Information | Understanding, being prepared | Researches everything before deciding; hates being caught unprepared |
| Recognition | Being seen and valued | Notices when effort or achievement goes unacknowledged |
| Experiences | A life that's felt and varied | Always proposing trips, new places, a story to tell; dreads routine |
| Freedom | Options and autonomy | Resists being boxed in or obligated |
| Growth | Becoming more | Always in a course, a book, a new practice; drawn to progress, not comfort |

**Lessons:** Core 22 The Values Introduction · 23 Values Overview · 24 Connection · 25 Information · 26 Recognition · 27 Experiences · 28 Freedom · 29 Growth

**Listening cue:** listen to the adjectives people use for those they admire. Whatever someone romanticizes is usually what they were deprived of. "She's so grounded" points to stability; "he's so free" points to freedom.

### Week 7: Putting it together

**Lessons:** Core 30 Needs, Decisions, Values Summary · Foundations 07 Needs, Decisions, Values Error Correction (lecture + reference PDF) · Foundations 08 The Common Mistakes People Make · Core 32 Wrap Up

The profile comes together as one sentence:

> **If I** [decision pillar], **then people will** [meet my need], **so I can finally** [value].

Example: "If I just stay strong for everybody, they'll keep me close, so I can finally feel connected."

Three things to listen for:

1. **Adjectives** reveal values (what they admire or romanticize).
2. **Justifications** reveal decision pillars (what they explain or defend).
3. **Repetitions** reveal needs (what they keep trying to get from the world, even when it doesn't work).

The error-correction PDF lists the common confusions, such as strength that's really approval, or detachment that's really acceptance. Work through it before the capstone.

**Practice:** build five profile sentences from real conversations. For each, note one alternative read you ruled out and why.

**Open question:** there is no Core lesson 31 in the source set. Confirm whether a lesson is missing.

## Unit 5: Authority and Self-Development (Week 8)

Unit 5 turns the lens on you. Reading people is half the Success Triangle; the self-mastery side decides whether anyone trusts what you say. This week you score your own authority, find your weakest area, and build a plan to fix it.

| Material | What it is | How you use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Hughes Authority Behavior Inventory (2024 edition) | A self-assessment that rates five traits from level 1 to 5 (Confidence, Discipline, Leadership, Gratitude, Enjoyment) and scores your habits 1–5 in five areas (Environment, Time, Appearance, Social, Financial) | Complete both parts honestly. Your goals for each trait sit in the level just above where you scored |
| The Authority Triangle | A one-page model of the inventory: internal behaviors, external effect, and underlying habits | Use it as the summary page for your results |
| Milgram obedience experiment | The classic study showing how readily ordinary people follow an authority figure | Discuss what made the authority believable, and how that responsibility applies to you |
| Limiting Beliefs worksheet | Prompts for spotting internal and external limiting beliefs, with sample lists on money, relationships, health, work, and self | Pick your two strongest beliefs and test them: Do I know an exception? Is this helping or limiting me? Does it put me in charge? |

**Key principle from the inventory:** fix weaknesses before building strengths. Your lowest trait has the biggest effect on how much influence you have.

**Practice:** complete the inventory and the limiting-beliefs worksheet. Then write a milestone plan with dated steps to raise your lowest trait and your lowest habit area by one level.

## Schedule and assessment

Assessment is based on practice, not recall. Logs and a field capstone count for most of the grade.

### Weekly schedule

| Week | Unit | Lessons | Deliverable |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Foundations | Five Lenses, Six Wound Types, Success Triangle | Stage-of-perception journal (3 entries) |
| 2 | Foundations | Childhood Development Triangle, Four Laws, Language | Triangle match for 3 people; Unit 1 check |
| 3 | Human Needs | Core 01–05 | Needs log (10 entries) |
| 4 | Human Needs | Core 06–10 | Needs log (10 entries); Unit 2 check |
| 5 | Decision Pillars | Core 11–21 | One request written for 3 pillars; Unit 3 check |
| 6 | Values | Core 22–29 | Values log (10 entries) |
| 7 | Integration | Core 30, 32; Error Correction; Common Mistakes | 5 profile sentences with ruled-out reads |
| 8 | Authority and Self-Development | Inventory, Authority Triangle, Milgram, Limiting Beliefs | Scored inventory + dated milestone plan; capstone due |

### Grading

| Component | Weight |
| --- | --- |
| Weekly practice logs | 40% |
| Unit checks (Units 1–3) | 20% |
| Capstone | 30% |
| Self-development plan | 10% |

### Capstone: three field profiles

Profile three people: one from a recorded interview and two from live conversations. For each one, submit:

1. The person's likely need, decision pillar, and value, each backed by the exact phrase that showed it.
2. Their profile sentence (*If I... then people will... so I can finally...*).
3. One alternative read you considered and why you ruled it out.
4. For the live profiles: what you said in response, how they reacted, and what you'd change next time.

A profile passes when the evidence phrases actually support the read and the response matches the need you identified.
